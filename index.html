      Strategy explainers

      Selling options for income, with a catch

      Covered calls and cash-secured puts both pay you a premium up front for taking on a stock's risk. On the same stock, strike and expiry the two have almost the same payoff, so they share the same catch: a large drop in the stock costs far more than the premium earns, and your gain is capped. The real examples below show each possible outcome for both.

      Covered calls

      A covered call means owning 100 shares and selling someone the right to buy them from you at a set price (the strike) by a set date. You collect a premium up front. In return, you keep the stock's downside and give up any gain above the strike. The three real examples below show each possible outcome.

      1. Own 100 shares. One option contract covers 100 shares, so this takes real money: about $55,900 for SPY in March 2025.
      2. Sell one call with a strike above today's price and collect the premium. It is yours to keep whatever happens next.
      3. At expiry, one of three things has happened: the stock stayed below the strike, rose above it, or fell.

      Cash-secured puts

      A cash-secured put means setting aside enough cash to buy 100 shares and selling someone the right to sell them to you at a set price (the strike) by a set date. You collect a premium up front. In return, if the stock falls below the strike you must buy the shares at the strike, however far the price has dropped, and if the stock soars you earn only the premium. The three real examples below use the same events as the covered calls.

      1. Set aside enough cash to buy 100 shares at the strike. For SPY at a $540 strike that was $54,000, tied up until expiry.
      2. Sell one put with a strike below today's price and collect the premium. It is yours to keep whatever happens next.
      3. At expiry, one of three things has happened: the stock stayed above the strike, soared, or fell below it and you had to buy the shares.

      FAQ

      Questions, answered

      Is AlphOpt investment advice?

      No. AlphOpt is an educational tool that explains how options and the Greeks behave, using historical examples. It does not recommend buying or selling any security, does not consider your finances, goals or risk tolerance, and is not a substitute for advice from a registered professional.

      Is AlphOpt registered with a securities regulator?

      No. AlphOpt is not registered as an adviser or dealer with any securities regulator, in Canada, the United States or elsewhere. It provides general education only and does not advise anyone on buying or selling securities. For advice about your own situation, speak with a registered adviser.

      Will AlphOpt tell me what to trade?

      No. AlphOpt does not publish trade ideas, signals or alerts. The case studies are historical examples chosen to teach how options behave, not trades to copy.

      Are the case studies real trades?

      No. The events and stock prices are real: actual daily closes, volumes and ranges from the time. The trades are hypothetical: we applied the same fixed teaching rules (+50% target, −35% stop, five sessions at most, checked at each close) with hindsight. No one placed them, and AlphOpt did not exist at the time. The events were chosen for their teaching value, not as a representative sample. Option premiums and Greeks are model estimates, not recorded quotes.

      Are options risky?

      Yes. Options are complex and can lose their entire value quickly, sometimes in a single day, and many people lose money trading them. Before trading options, read the options risk disclosure your broker provides and make sure you understand it.

      Do I need to understand options already?

      No. Each case explains the contract in plain language: what it cost, the most it could lose, and what the stock needed to do. The Greeks lab lets you explore “what if the stock drops?” or “what if a week passes?”

      What data do you collect?

      Only the email address you give us to join the waitlist, with a record of your consent. Your language choice is saved in your own browser. There are no ads, analytics or tracking cookies. See the privacy policy.

      Which stocks does AlphOpt cover?

      Ten: SPY, QQQ and IWM (ETFs), plus Apple (AAPL), Amazon (AMZN), AMD, Meta (META), Microsoft (MSFT), NVIDIA (NVDA) and Tesla (TSLA). We chose them because their options are among the most actively traded in the U.S., not because we expect them to rise or consider them good investments. Large, well-known stocks can still fall sharply.

      Are covered calls or cash-secured puts low risk?

      Not in the way a GIC is. With a covered call you own the shares; with a cash-secured put you have promised to buy them at the strike. Either way you carry most of the stock's downside: the premium offsets only a small part of a fall, and your gain is capped. On the same stock, strike and expiry the two have almost the same payoff. Both can lose money, and neither always beats a GIC. The examples on this page show every outcome.
